Kingsdown House
Two-storey rear extension

A considered addition to a Victorian end-of-terrace above the harbour — full-height glazing to the garden, a new family kitchen, and a first-floor bathroom regrouping.

Prepared forRavi & Priya Patel
Address14 Kingsdown Parade, Bristol BS6
Date6 April 2026
Valid until6 May 2026
Section01

Understanding

You've lived in Kingsdown for eleven years and have outgrown the ground floor. The kitchen is dark, the dining space unusable for six of you at once, and the rear yard sits three steps below the house — inconvenient, and cutting the garden off from daily life.

You want a light, generous family room open to the garden; a proper utility separate from the kitchen; and — if the numbers work — a small ensuite off the master bedroom on the first floor. You have permitted development potential for the ground floor and a Bristol conservation-area consent to be handled carefully at planning.

The house's brick and its bay window are part of what you love about it. Nothing we design will pretend they aren't there.

Section02

Approach

We work in RIBA stages, and we'll take you through concept, planning, technical design and tender in that order — showing you real options at each step rather than one preferred answer. You'll see three concept sketches at Stage 2, and we'll agree the direction with you before any drawing gets tightened up.

We keep the ground-floor rear low and horizontal to sit under the eaves line of the original house. Materials are drawn from what's already on the terrace — the same brick, a zinc roof, and slim aluminium glazing. The intent is that the addition reads as quietly obvious a hundred years from now.

whole-house renovation

A patient restoration of a Grade II listed Cotswold stone farmhouse, with a new garden room replacing a lean-to and a considered internal reordering to bring light through.

Prepared forThe Hollis-Marchant Family
AddressRooksmoor Lane, Painswick GL6
Date14 March 2026
Valid until14 April 2026
Section01

Understanding

Rooksmoor is a house that already knows what it wants to be. The 1780 core is honest and quiet. The 1970 kitchen wing at the back is honest about being neither of those things. Our first job is to remove enough of the recent additions that the older house can breathe again.

You've asked for a garden room that connects the family properly to the paddock, a rebuilt utility, and a reworking of the first-floor bedrooms so the children have proper rooms without touching the front elevation.

Section02

Approach

Listed building consent is the fulcrum of this project. We'll spend Stage 2 documenting what's original and what isn't, and we'll bring the conservation officer into the conversation early — before you and I commit to a scheme they might quietly hate.

The new garden room reads as a piece of furniture set against the older house: oak frame, standing-seam roof, all glazing the same slim profile as your existing sash windows. No pastiche, no showing off.

Meridian House
Cat-B fit-out, 4th floor

A 720 sq m workspace for Northmark Digital in Kings Cross: an open floor with acoustic control, three enclosed rooms, and a café-height bar to the north window line.

CLIENTNorthmark Digital Ltd
SITEMeridian House, York Way N1
ISSUED2026·03·11
VALID2026·04·11
SECTION01

Understanding

Northmark are relocating from Old Street on a five-year lease. The brief prioritises heads-down space over meeting rooms — an inversion of what the previous tenant left behind. The floor arrives shell-and-core in July, and you'd like to be operating in September.

Your product team runs quiet; sales runs loud. Both use the same floor. Acoustic separation done properly is the single largest technical constraint, and the one we'd like your endorsement of the strategy on before we get into layout choices.

SECTION02

Approach

We work in RIBA stages compressed for commercial fit-out — Stages 2, 3 and 4 running with a ten-week critical path against your September occupation date. A single named lead from our side, a single named lead from yours.

The commercial answer is a strong acoustic backbone (raised carpet, ceiling clouds over open-plan, closed rooms detailed to STC 45) so the front-of-house materials can be as light as they'd like to be.
